Botox for Chronic Migraine

Botox (onabotulinumtoxinA) is an FDA-approved preventive treatment for chronic migraine. It works by modifying the release of neurotransmitters involved in pain signaling.

Monti considers Botox for adults with a pattern consistent with chronic migraine. The FDA-approved indication covers adults with 15 or more headache days per month, with headaches lasting at least four hours per day. A provider can assess whether it is appropriate for you.

Treatment is typically performed every 12 weeks using the PREEMPT injection protocol.

In the pooled PREEMPT trials involving 1,384 patients, 44.8% of patients receiving Botox achieved at least a 50% reduction in headache days, compared with 34.2% receiving placebo. Some patients respond after their second or third treatment cycle.

  • Fewer headache days and less severe migraines
  • Less headache-related disability and improved quality of life
  • Potentially less reliance on rescue medications

Reported side effects include neck pain (9%), headache (5%), eyelid drooping (4%), muscle weakness (4%), and injection-site pain (3%).

Rare but serious complications can include allergic reactions and spread of toxin effects, which may cause swallowing or breathing difficulties.

Vyepti Migraine Infusions

Vyepti (eptinezumab-jjmr) is an FDA-approved intravenous preventive migraine medication. It targets calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP), a signaling molecule involved in migraine.

Adults with episodic or chronic migraine may be candidates for preventive CGRP-targeted treatment. Your provider can help assess whether Vyepti is appropriate for you.

Vyepti is given by IV infusion approximately every three months. The recommended dose is 100 mg, and some patients may benefit from 300 mg. The infusion takes about 30 minutes.

In PROMISE-2, a clinical trial involving 1,072 patients with chronic migraine, average monthly migraine days decreased:

  • From 16.1 to 8.5 days with 100 mg
  • From 16.1 to 7.9 days with 300 mg
  • From 16.2 to 10.5 days with placebo

The reductions compared with placebo were statistically significant.

  • Fewer monthly migraine days and reduced migraine severity
  • Potentially rapid onset of preventive benefit
  • Improved daily functioning
  • Quarterly administration

In clinical trials, nasopharyngitis was reported in 6% of patients receiving 100 mg and 8% receiving 300 mg. Hypersensitivity reactions occurred in approximately 1% and 2%, respectively.

Other important safety considerations include allergic reactions, constipation with serious complications, new or worsening high blood pressure, and Raynaud’s phenomenon.

Botox or Vyepti: Which is right for me?

Botox modifies peripheral pain signaling through targeted injections. Vyepti blocks CGRP activity through intravenous treatment.

Monti considers your migraine history, symptoms, previous treatment response, medical history, and goals. For some patients with difficult-to-control chronic migraine, the care team may consider combination treatment under medical supervision.
