How to Schedule a Medical Aesthetic Consultation in 2026

Booking a medical aesthetic consultation in 2026 takes less time than most people expect: choose a physician-led clinic, confirm the provider's credentials, request an appointment through the clinic's booking portal or phone line, and prepare a short list of questions before you arrive. This guide from Monti Institute walks through each step, from first search to signed treatment plan.

Here's the part most guides skip. A consultation is not a sales pitch you sit through. It is a medical appointment, and the quality of the provider you choose determines everything that follows.

The seven tips below cover what to prepare, what to ask, what should make you walk away, and how to compare fees without getting misled. Read them before you book anything.

What to Expect at a Med Spa Consultation

A med spa consultation is a structured medical evaluation, not a quick chat. Most practitioners walk through the same five stages, and knowing them in advance lets you tell a thorough visit from a rushed one.

1. Intake and health history review. You will complete a medical questionnaire and then discuss it with the clinician. Expect questions about medications, supplements, allergies, prior aesthetic treatments and their dates, bleeding or autoimmune conditions, and, importantly, neurological conditions such as chronic migraine. Migraine history affects which treatments a responsible clinician will recommend, so raise it early rather than at the end.

2. Physical assessment. The clinician examines your skin and facial anatomy, often with good lighting and sometimes with imaging or skin-analysis tools. This is where candidacy is actually determined. A provider who recommends a treatment before this step has skipped the part that makes the recommendation medical.

3. Treatment discussion. You should hear options, including doing nothing, with realistic outcomes, downtime, risks, and alternatives. Ask how many times the clinician has performed the specific treatment on anatomy like yours. A good answer is specific; a vague one is a signal.

4. Consent and written plan. Before any treatment, you should receive a written plan and a consent form that names the risks. Read the consent form. If it is missing complications you discussed, ask why. The FDA's consumer information on dermal fillers is clear that these are medical products with real risks, including vascular complications, and the consent conversation should reflect that.

5. Follow-up and aftercare. Confirm who you contact if something goes wrong, how after-hours concerns are handled, and whether follow-up visits are included. Get the aftercare instructions in writing before you leave.

Key Takeaway Appointment length varies by clinic and by how many concerns you raise. You should leave the room understanding three things: what was recommended, why it suits your anatomy and health history, and what could go wrong. If any of those is missing, ask before you book a treatment.

Bring your medication list and any relevant medical history to the visit. The more complete your history, the more specific the plan, and the fewer surprises when you sit down to compare fees.

Your Aesthetic Consultation Checklist: What to Bring and Prepare

Preparation is where most patients lose use. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons guidance on choosing a provider recommends verifying board certification before any procedure. Do that homework before the appointment, not after.

  • Photo ID and insurance card, if applicable
  • A complete list of medications, supplements, and allergies
  • Your medical history, including migraine, autoimmune, or bleeding disorders
  • Names of any prior aesthetic treatments and dates
  • Three specific concerns written down, ranked by priority
  • A budget range and a clear line you will not cross
  • Questions copied from the list below
  • A note on your calendar for the cancellation window

Two practical notes. Skip alcohol for a day or two before the visit, since it affects bruising risk for injectables. And arrive without heavy makeup if you want an accurate skin assessment.

Cosmetic Consultation Questions to Ask Before You Book

Ask about credentials, experience with your specific concern, risks, alternatives, and total cost. Write them down and work through them out loud.

  1. Are you board-certified, and in which specialty?
  2. How many times have you performed this specific treatment?
  3. What are the realistic risks and side effects for my anatomy?
  4. What happens if I am not a good candidate?
  5. What alternatives exist, including doing nothing?
  6. What is the complete cost, including follow-up visits?
  7. What is your policy on revisions and complications?
  8. Who handles emergencies, and how do I reach them?

A clinician who answers these without flinching is a good sign. One who deflects or rushes is telling you something useful.

Cosmetic Consultation Red Flags: How to Spot an Unqualified Provider

Walk away if the provider cannot or will not confirm board certification, pushes a treatment before examining you, or quotes a price before taking a health history. Those three behaviors are the clearest cosmetic consultation red flags.

Watch for these as well:

  • Pressure to book and pay a deposit the same day
  • No mention of risks, downtime, or alternatives
  • Treatments offered by staff with no clinical credentials
  • Vague answers about who performs the procedure
  • No written consent form or aftercare instructions
  • A clinic that will not discuss complications

The FDA's consumer information on dermal fillers is clear that these are medical products with real risks, including vascular complications. Any provider who frames injectables as risk-free is either uninformed or not being straight with you.

How to Compare Consultation Fees and Understand What Is Included

Consultation fees vary widely, and a low fee is not automatically the better deal. The number on the booking page tells you almost nothing until you know which fee model the clinic uses and what the fee buys you. Consultation fee arrangements commonly fall into several models, and each one can change your true total cost in a different way.

Fee model How it works What to watch
Flat consultation fee You pay a set amount at booking, separate from treatment Ask whether the fee is credited if you proceed
Credited consultation Fee is applied toward treatment booked within a set window Confirm the window, commonly 30 to 90 days, and whether it resets
Free consultation No charge, often with a same-day booking incentive Free can mean a shorter assessment or a sales-forward visit
Tiered fee Higher fee for physician-led or multi-concern assessments Confirm who actually performs the consult before you pay

A credited fee is only a discount if you book treatment. If you are still deciding, a flat fee with no strings is often the cleaner comparison. Ask each clinic directly:

  • Is the fee credited toward treatment, and within what window?
  • Who performs the consult, physician, nurse practitioner, or sales staff?
  • Is a written treatment plan included, or is that a separate visit?
  • Are follow-up visits bundled or billed per visit?
  • What is the deposit and cancellation policy if I reschedule?

That last question matters more than most patients expect. A deposit that is non-refundable inside a short cancellation window can cost you more than the difference between two consultation fees. Get the cancellation window in writing before you pay anything.

Pro Tip Calculate true total cost, not the headline fee: consultation fee minus any credit, plus deposit risk, plus the cost of follow-up visits the plan requires. A $0 consult with a non-refundable deposit and two paid follow-ups can cost more than a flat fee that includes a written plan and one follow-up.

Ask whether the quoted price is the total or a starting point, since a quote given before a health history and physical assessment may not reflect your full plan. Pricing depends on assessment, anatomy, and the treatments selected. Visit the Monti Institute website or request a consultation for current pricing specific to you.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I schedule a medical aesthetic consultation?

You can schedule a medical aesthetic consultation by calling the clinic directly, using its online booking portal, or submitting an appointment request through the practice website. Most physician-led offices ask for your name, contact details, the service you are interested in, and your preferred appointment times. Some clinics also request a brief medical history form before your visit. At Monti Institute, you can request an appointment online or by phone, and the team will confirm availability and explain any preparation steps before your in-person visit.

What should I ask during an aesthetic consultation?

Ask about the provider's credentials and training, how many procedures of your type they perform regularly, and what results are realistic for your anatomy. Request a clear explanation of risks, recovery time, and alternatives, including the option of doing nothing. Confirm who will perform the treatment and whether a physician supervises the plan. Also ask about total costs, follow-up visits, and the clinic's policy for revisions or complications. Bringing a written list of cosmetic consultation questions helps you compare answers without feeling rushed.

How long does an aesthetic consultation take?

A thorough aesthetic consultation length depends on the number of concerns you discuss and whether a physical examination or imaging is needed. First visits tend to run longer because they include a medical history review, skin or facial assessment, and a discussion of treatment options. If you plan to cover several concerns, such as injectables and laser therapy, ask for a longer slot when you book. Appointment duration varies by clinic, so confirm the expected time when you schedule.

What are red flags during a cosmetic consultation?

Watch for pressure to book a treatment the same day, vague answers about who will perform the procedure, or a provider who dismisses your questions about risks and alternatives. Other cosmetic consultation red flags include no visible credentials or licensing, a clinic that avoids discussing total costs, and staff who promise guaranteed or permanent results. A qualified provider will welcome questions, explain limitations honestly, and encourage you to take time to decide. If something feels off, seek a second opinion before committing.
